# Late Cretaceous assumptions

The campaign opens at 04:00 on 1 September, 68 million years ago (Maastrichtian). The Rhenish Massif and Holy Cross Island are at war. The Carpathian Ridge is neutral. Every other landmass drawn on the map is neutral. There were no human states in this age: the names are the Late Cretaceous landmasses that occupied those present-day polygons. Province shapes stay present-day administrative areas. There is no separate Danzig state in this scenario: the Natural Earth extract has no Free City polygon, and present-day Germany has no detached East Prussia.

Province borders are present-day administrative areas. Rhenish supply stays inside the Massif. It does not cross Holy Cross Island, and it does not use a corridor that the current coastline does not have.

Populations and factory counts are rounded national totals, not a factory-by-factory census. Civilian and military factories start from those totals and then change when construction finishes. Each country with military factories begins the campaign producing harness. Dockyards are stored and shown. They do not build ships. The Rhenish Massif starts with beast handling already known. Holy Cross Island and the Carpathian Ridge start with an empty lab. Computer-controlled countries fill empty research slots at midnight. The Massif, the Island, and the Ridge each have an original focus tree. Other countries have none. Monthly manpower growth from population is not running. The manpower number is the trained handler pool.

The economy settles at midnight. The opening hours of 1 September, from 04:00, do not produce or extract anything. A decision that pauses the clock at midnight does so after that night's settlement.

Harness in a pack is the pack's own stock. The national stockpile is separate. Recruiting a new pack spends handlers and harness from those national pools and starts the formation at the template's full requirement. Packs already in the field do not draw reinforcements from the pools. The Kraków Group starts short of harness, so its readiness is below full strength. The Galician Detachment sits on a province with no railway and infrastructure 1, so its supply ratio is below one. The starting template is twelve hadrosaurs. Its combat statistics are the sum of those beasts. Line species are hadrosaur, raptor, ceratops, tyrannosaur, and ankylosaur. Support species are stegosaur, pterosaur, and spinosaur.

Provinces with infrastructure of 5 start at railway level 2. Infrastructure of 3 or 4 starts at level 1. A province on the outer edge of the land mesh is a seaport and starts with a level 1 port. Capitals start as supply hubs. These levels are a schematic, not a surveyed Cretaceous network.

Province resources are a short stand-in: steel in North Rhine-Westphalia and Silesia, and oil in Subcarpathia. Rubber is not on the map. Countries hold a rubber stockpile instead. The resource map mode leaves a province gray when it has no deposit.

The controller of a deposit receives its daily amount. The Rhenish Massif and Holy Cross Island are at war, so they cannot trade. The Carpathian Ridge can sell tungsten, and any partner can sell a resource it actually has in stock. Import volume is also capped by connected infrastructure. A sea blockade is not simulated.

September weather is clear everywhere on this map. Forts start at zero. Terrain affects defense. This import does not mark a river tag. Air power is not simulated. Armor, piercing, entrenchment, forts, weather, and encirclement do change combat once a battle starts.

The satellite-pack decision on 2 September is original writing. It changes Rhenish manpower, stability, war support, or the harness stockpile, depending on the choice. Weights are 70 and 30 for a computer-controlled Massif. The player is paused and chooses.

Ownership and control are separate. Capturing an empty province or winning a battle changes the controller only.
